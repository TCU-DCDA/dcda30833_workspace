Working from Examples

In this course, an example is a system you can take apart, not an image to reproduce. I will always show you how something was made; I will rarely show you one right answer to match.

What you'll be shown

  • Rules, not just results. Wherever possible, examples come with their system exposed: Sol LeWitt's instruction texts, Tyler Hobbs's Flow Fields write-up, Casey Reas's Software Structures. The method is the point.
  • A range, not a single piece. You'll often see many outputs of one system side by side. The system is the work; the output varies.
  • Process and failure. Rough sketches, dead ends, live demos built badly on purpose. These show what working looks like, not what finished looks like.
  • Precedents to take apart. Each project's System Autopsy asks you to recover the rules behind a precedent work. Looking at an example here means analyzing it, not matching it.

What you can always ask for

Ask any time about scope and format: how big, how finished, how long the reflection runs, how files are organized. Those questions have plain answers and you'll get them.

Working from an example without submitting it

Learning from other people's code is how this discipline works. The line is between building on a system and handing someone else's system in as yours.

  • Recover the rule, then change it. Your work should start from a rule you can state in a sentence. If you started from someone else's rule, alter it until it produces behavior theirs doesn't.
  • Keep nothing you can't explain. The Explanation Test from the AI policy applies to every source: you must be able to explain every line you submit.
  • Credit your sources. In your README.md, name any sketch, tutorial or artwork you worked from, link it, and say what you took and what you changed. This is the same practice the AI policy asks for when you use AI.

See also Collaboration in Computer Art: you are always collaborating with the medium, the community and art history. What matters is whether you engage with what you borrow or just paste it.
